Adding up the economic effects of immigration

New shores bring skills, both high and low,
An economic tide does grow.
Wages shift, debates ignite,
Immigration’s worth, a complex fight.

publication

Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis The Regional Economist

Summary

This article examines the economic effects of immigration in the United States, focusing on labor market impacts and fiscal implications. While immigrants increase the diversity of skills, enabling economic expansion and boosting productivity, their influx can exert downward pressure on wages for low-skilled U.S.-born workers. Studies present mixed findings: Borjas emphasizes significant wage declines for low-skilled natives, while Ottaviano and Peri argue that the benefits to other skill classes and productivity offset these losses, leading to overall wage gains. The fiscal impact is nuanced, as younger immigrants with higher fertility rates provide future fiscal benefits, but low-skilled immigrants initially contribute less in taxes. The speed and success of immigrant assimilation, particularly for Mexican-born workers, are crucial to determining the long-term net effects of immigration.

Keywords
